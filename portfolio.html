Ascent

Project Overview: A Personal Operating System

Ascent is a single user web application I designed and built to run my academic and working life from one place. It is written in Next.js, backed by a Neon Postgres database and deployed on Vercel, and it installs as a progressive web app so that it behaves the same on my phone, my iPad and my laptop.

  • Work and invoicing: hours captured per employer and per student, outstanding totals for each pay period, and invoice PDFs generated on demand.
  • Marks: weighted tracking across my four modules, including the separate final examination hurdle, with a progress report export.
  • Focus: a pomodoro timer that logs every session to the database and survives navigation and app closure, with streaks derived from the session log.
  • Tasks: deadline tracking with push a day and push a week controls, fed one way from my Notion archive so that there is a single source of truth.

What Made It Difficult

The features were the easy part. The real problems were timer state that has to survive a closed browser tab, three pay periods that align neither with each other nor with a calendar month, and keeping data honest when it lives in two systems at once. Solving those taught me more about building software than any tutorial has.

Litigation Tracker

Project Overview: Case Management for a Law Firm

A small South African law firm was tracking its entire litigation caseload in a single HTML file. It ran on one computer, stored everything in that browser, and could only export a basic CSV. The principal attorney needed something he and a colleague in another location could both read and update, that would tell them when something changed without flooding their inboxes, and that would show at a glance how far each matter had progressed and what came next. Because the system holds privileged client information, security was not optional.

What I Built

I designed and delivered a multi user web application to replace it, built with Next.js, TypeScript and PostgreSQL and deployed on Vercel. The firm handles three kinds of matter, plaintiff, defendant and general, and each follows its own procedure. Plaintiff matters branch depending on whether the other side files a notice of intention to defend. I modelled these workflows in a single configuration file, so the firm's process can change without touching the rest of the code, and each matter keeps its own copy of its steps, so editing a workflow never rewrites a case's history.

  • Progress at a glance: a horizontal step tracker, carried over from the tool the client already liked, working fully on a phone.
  • Accountability: a full audit trail of who changed what and when.
  • Reporting: filtered PDF and CSV reports, plus a private calendar feed of upcoming deadlines that subscribes into Outlook or Google Calendar.
  • Notifications: a single grouped digest rather than an email per change, respecting quiet hours and never reporting a user's own edits.
  • Security: invite only accounts, passwords hashed with scrypt, sessions held server side so access can be revoked instantly, rate limited logins and security headers on every response.

What I Learned

The hardest part came after the first demo, when the client could not sign in. A full end to end testing pass turned up problems that had looked fine on the surface. Switching a case between the two plaintiff routes merged both routes into one nonsensical list. A timezone error made cases due today read as due tomorrow. The calendar feed broke the iCalendar specification on multi byte characters. And code pushes had never been deploying at all. Fixing those, and verifying each fix rather than assuming it worked, taught me more than the initial build did. The system now runs as a live demo while the firm decides on hosting and a subscription plan.

Rowing Treasury

Wits University Boat Club and USSA Rowing

I have been Treasurer of the Wits University Boat Club since October 2025 and continue in the role through 2027. From 2027 I also hold the Treasurer seat on the USSA Rowing Executive Committee, for a term running to 2030. The club carries more than 40 athletes, and I have been accountable for over R1 million in club funds across this season.

  • Boat Race campaign: budgeting, setting and collecting athlete contributions, and reconciling four payment streams across camp, kit, USSA entries and squad racing suits.
  • Procurement: equipment quotes and orders through Wits Sport, covering ergometers, sweep oars, accessories and boat repair.
  • Governance: chairing the financial side of formal committee meetings, producing official minutes and maintaining an action items schedule.
  • Events and logistics: a catering brief and full reconciliation for the winter training camp, a bulk kit order for the squad, and a fundraising quiz night.

This is ordinary accounting done under real conditions: many small payers, incomplete records, deadlines that do not move, and athletes who deserve a straight answer about exactly what they owe and why. It has taught me more about controls and communication than any set of lecture notes.

Taxation Project

Project Overview: VAT on a Student Accommodation Development

A group project analysing the value added tax consequences of a student accommodation development in Parktown. We worked through the Supreme Court of Appeal decision in Respublica, applied its reasoning to facts that did not map onto it cleanly, and presented our conclusions as a video submission.

  • Characterisation: tested the supply against the commercial accommodation provisions of the VAT Act rather than assuming the answer.
  • Case application: separated what Respublica actually decided from what it is often said to decide, and reasoned from the former.
  • Consequences: set out the input tax position and the practical exposure for the developer if the characterisation were challenged.

BDO Project

Project Overview: BioCane Business Model

The project focused on establishing BioCane, a business producing bioplastic from sugarcane, aiming to contribute to sustainable development. The business was situated in the Somkhele mining community in KwaZulu-Natal, which faced economic decline due to the closure of a local mine. BioCane sought to:

  • Leverage local resources: Partnered with a local sugarcane farmer for raw materials.
  • Tackle environmental challenges: Addressed the environmental impact of traditional plastic by producing biodegradable plastics from sugarcane, reducing pollution.
  • Job creation: BioCane aimed to revitalize the local economy by creating jobs in the production facility and implementing sustainable energy solutions such as solar panels and boreholes to support both production and the community.

Download the BDO Project Case Study below.

Case Study

Project Execution

I worked in a group of six members, each managing different business functions. The project included financial planning with a 5-year budget, operations delegation, SWOT and PESTELE analyses, risk management, and compliance with sustainable development goals (SDGs).

Final Project Submission

The final submission for the BioCane project included a comprehensive set of documents that outlined every aspect of the business model. The cover letter, cover page, and contents page introduced the submission. The ownership and management section detailed the structure of the business, followed by the vision and mission statements that set BioCane’s long-term goals. The executive summary provided a concise overview of the entire business plan. In-depth sections like the market strategy, technology use, and SWOT analysis showcased the competitive advantage and operational model. The PESTELE analysis (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal, and Ethical) and triple bottom line addressed how the business aligned with sustainable development. Shareholder analysis and positioning in the industry for growth further reinforced BioCane’s potential. The project also analyzed the SDGs addressed (Sustainable Development Goals), global and South African competitors, and included a risk analysis. Detailed sections on the target market, operations delegation, and a 5-year budget (covering costs, revenues, and profits) offered financial projections. Additionally, we incorporated insights from ChatGPT responses to refine our strategy. The project concluded with a detailed cost breakdown and supporting references. Deloitte CA of the Future Project

Project Overview: AI in Shipping for Smith’s (Pty) Ltd

In this project, you and your team developed a digital transformation strategy for Smith’s, a chocolate factory, focusing on incorporating Artificial Intelligence (AI) into the company's shipping process. The project was part of the Deloitte CA of the Future initiative, which aimed to expose students to the SAICA CA (2025) framework, emphasizing digital disruption, value creation, critical thinking, and integrated thinking.

Download the Deloitte CA(SA) of the Future Project Case Study below.

Case Study

Project Execution

  1. AI Integration in the Shipping Process:
    • I explored how AI technologies can be used to digitize Smith's existing shipping and invoicing process. This included automating order management, inventory tracking, delivery verification, and payment processing using AI-driven tools.
    • Key technologies involved:
      • AI-driven inventory management for tracking stock levels.
      • Automated invoicing systems.
      • Smart delivery tracking for real-time updates.
    • These implementations were designed to increase operational efficiency, reduce manual errors, and streamline the revenue business process.
  2. Risk Assessment:

    We conducted a thorough risk analysis on the digitization of the shipping process. The risks identified included cybersecurity threats, system failures, and the potential for AI miscommunication, impacting the triple bottom line (financial, social, and environmental factors).

  3. Employee Training for AI:

    Recognizing the risks of staff not fully understanding AI, the project emphasized the need for employee training. We proposed tailored programs to upskill employees, allowing them to effectively leverage AI in daily operations.

  4. IAS 2 and Inventory Costs:

    The project also applied IAS 2 to identify the costs of purchase and conversion involved in producing Smith's chocolate bars. You provided four clear examples of these costs and how they are accounted for under the standard.

Final Project Submission

My group and I presented the project in the form of a report and a 10-minute presentation, as required by the given case study(above). The report covered our digital strategy, risk analysis, and recommendations for employee training and inventory cost management, while the presentation used slides and visual aids to engage the marker. This project demonstrates my ability to apply digital transformation strategies to real-world business problems, showing how AI and other technologies can drive value in traditional industries.
